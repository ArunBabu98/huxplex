# 12 — Impact on Humans (the Individual)

> Cross-cutting, zoomed all the way in: not "society," but *you* — your agency, your attention,
> your trust, your records, your relationship to the AI acting on your behalf. The macro effects
> in [11](11-impact-on-society.md) are felt one person at a time, here.

## The individual's day, if this works

A person in a mature-Huxplex world:

- **Owns their identity and records.** Their credentials, health consent, learning history, and
  reputation are theirs — portable, verifiable, not trapped in institutional silos.
- **Delegates to agents on a leash.** Their AI assistants act with real autonomy but under
  **Work Visas** the person sets and can revoke instantly: spend ceilings, scopes, expiry. "My
  agent booked it" without "my agent drained my account."
- **Can always say no.** The **biological veto** means consequential machine action is haltable
  by verified humans — a felt sense of *not being at the mercy of the machines*.
- **Gets paid for what was previously taken.** Data, attention, and creative work become licensed
  and accounted rather than silently harvested.
- **Trusts what they see again.** Provenance lets them tell the real photo from the deepfake, the
  real credential from the forgery.

## The same day, if it goes wrong

- **Consent fatigue & rubber-stamping.** Endless Visa approvals and veto windows → people click
  "approve all," and the leash becomes decorative. The veto exists but is never meaningfully used.
- **The permanent record.** Soulbound reputation means **mistakes never fade**. A bad year, a
  wrongful mark, an unpopular view — etched into a quantum-durable ledger with no forgetting.
- **Personhood as a checkpoint.** If proving you're human becomes a gate for economic
  participation, the unverified (the marginalized, the privacy-conscious, the
  wrongly-excluded) are locked out — a new and brutal form of exclusion.
- **Cognitive offloading & deskilling.** When agents do the thinking, human capability can
  atrophy; "non-redundant contribution" scoring could even *price* people by how much novel
  thought they still produce — a dystopian read of the merit idea.
- **Surveillance, if personhood is done wrong.** Biometric capture turns "sovereignty" into a
  panopticon (see risk #8).

## The tensions, made personal

| You gain | You risk | Depends on getting right |
|---|---|---|
| Control over your data & identity | A permanent, unforgiving dossier | Decay, appeal, right-to-erasure design |
| Powerful agents working for you | Approval fatigue → meaningless veto | UX that keeps oversight *real*, not constant |
| Provable authenticity | "Unsigned = untrue" chills the informal | Provenance as optional signal, not a gate |
| Pay for your data/work | Pressure to commodify *everything* about yourself | Floors, opt-outs, dignity baselines |
| Verified humanity (anti-Sybil) | Exclusion of the unverified | Privacy-preserving, universally accessible personhood |

## The design imperatives this implies

For the human-level impact to be net-positive, several things are **non-negotiable** and should
be treated as product requirements, not nice-to-haves:

1. **Forgetting and forgiveness** must be designed into soulbound reputation — decay curves,
   appeal, expungement. Permanence without mercy is cruelty at protocol scale.
2. **The veto must stay meaningful** — oversight surfaced rarely and legibly, not drowned in
   approvals. A leash you never feel is not a leash.
3. **Personhood must never become a wall** — a dignity/identity floor (an unrevokable baseline,
   [I10](03-identity-personhood-sovereignty.md)) so no human is *deleted* from participation.
4. **Privacy by construction** — no raw biometrics, selective disclosure everywhere, ZK by default.
5. **A right not to be measured** — the novelty/merit machinery must be opt-in; no human should be
   *forced* to have their contribution scored to participate ([ADR-0007](../adr/0007-sentience-framing.md)).

## Net assessment

At the individual level, Huxplex's instincts are unusually humane *on paper* — sovereignty,
ownership, accountability of machines to people. The danger is that every one of those goods has
a near-identical evil twin (sovereignty→surveillance, merit→caste, accountability→approval
fatigue, authenticity→gatekeeping). The difference between the two futures is almost entirely in
*details the protocol must get right* — forgetting, accessibility, opt-in, UX of oversight —
rather than in the headline thesis.

### Open Questions
- How do you keep human oversight *cognitively real* rather than fatigued into rubber-stamping?
- What is the humane half-life of a soulbound mark, and who adjudicates expungement?
- Can personhood be universal and privacy-preserving enough that *no one* is excluded by it?
