# 11 — Impact on Society

> Cross-cutting. If even a fraction of the use cases land, Huxplex doesn't just process
> transactions — it reshapes labor, power, institutions, and the distribution of value. This
> file is the honest reckoning of those second-order effects: the good, the dangerous, and the
> genuinely uncertain. It is normative where the rest of the catalogue is technical.

## The central social question

Huxplex is a bet that **autonomous AI agents will become major economic actors**, and that the
right response is not to ban them from the economy but to give them a *neutral, accountable
substrate* with **human sovereignty wired in**. Every societal impact below flows from that bet.
The alternative futures it implicitly argues against are: (a) agents transacting on
human-hostile, unaccountable rails, or (b) the machine economy captured inside a few corporate
walled gardens. Whether a neutral public substrate is better — and achievable — is the wager.

## Three honest tensions

### 1. Empowerment vs. displacement (labor)
The optimistic story: humans get **new income** (licensing data, providing oversight, exercising
governance) and agents do drudgery. The pessimistic story: once agents do the work and humans
only *approve* it, human economic value collapses into a thin "veto-clicker" layer that's easy to
automate away or rubber-stamp. Huxplex's design choice — making human authority a *hard
constraint* rather than a courtesy — is the strongest available hedge, but it cannot by itself
guarantee humans retain *meaningful* (not merely formal) economic agency. That's a policy and
social question the protocol can enable but not solve.

### 2. Merit vs. caste (inequality)
Soulbound merit (SNTNC) is pitched as fairer than capital: you earn standing by contributing, and
you can't buy it. But a non-transferable, permanent score can calcify into a **caste system** —
early advantages compound, mistakes never fade, and those with more compute simply contribute
(and score) more. Without designed-in decay, forgiveness, appeal, and floors, "merit" can entrench
inequality more durably than money, which at least can be redistributed. This is why the
[philosophical file](13-philosophical-implications.md) treats merit measurement as the project's
most ethically loaded mechanism.

### 3. Sovereignty vs. surveillance (power)
The whole "human sovereignty" thesis hinges on **proof-of-personhood**. The naive implementation
— biometric DIDs — would build the most complete, quantum-durable surveillance asset in history,
inverting the entire project into its opposite (risk #8). Society gets the upside (machines stay
accountable to verified humans) only if the privacy-preserving ZK path works. If it doesn't,
Huxplex should not ship personhood at all — the cure would be worse than the disease.

## Net assessment

The **least speculative, most clearly positive** societal contributions are the unglamorous ones:
durable integrity for records that must survive decades (deeds, health, science, heritage) and a
clean post-quantum migration path for critical systems. The **highest-upside but most uncertain**
are the agent-economy and sovereignty mechanisms — they could either keep an AI-saturated economy
accountable to humans, or fail in ways that concentrate power. The catalogue is deliberately
sequenced so the safe wins fund the risky bets, never the reverse.

### Open Questions
- Can the human veto preserve *substantive* economic agency, or only formal veto power that
  atrophies into rubber-stamping?
- Is soulbound merit net-egalitarian or net-entrenching, and which mechanisms (decay, floors,
  appeal) decide that?
- Does a neutral global substrate reduce geopolitical friction or become a new object of it?
